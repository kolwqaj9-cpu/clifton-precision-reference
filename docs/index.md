## 5. Die Life, Maintenance, and Part Consistency

Preserved shop record for cliftonprecision.com. The former plant is not quoting from this domain. The sections above describe precision metal stamping and sheet metal work in the shop's own process language, and this closing note stays in that language to cover the part of the trade that runs behind every stamped part: the die. A stamping die is a capital tool with a working life measured in strokes, and a precision stamp shop lives or falls by how it manages that life. Die sharpening schedules, punch and button wear, stripper condition, and guide alignment decide whether the ten-thousandth part still matches the first. In short-run work the arithmetic is different but the discipline is the same. Setup time and tryout dominate a small order, so the shop that quotes short runs competitively is the one whose dies go in, prove out, and come off the press without drama. The same thinking extends to sheet metal fabrication, where tooling means punch press tooling, press brake dies, and forming fixtures rather than progressive dies, but where setup discipline decides cost just the same. Inspection closes the loop. A precision shop checks first articles against the print, holds the run to the same standard, and treats burr condition, flatness, and hole location as part of the product rather than as afterthoughts. The preserved pages describe a supplier that customers could hand a drawing to, and the drawing, not a catalog, defined the job. Reading the record as a whole, the archive is about controlled tooling, controlled setups, and controlled inspection, which is what the word precision means when it is attached to stamping and fabrication rather than to marketing.
